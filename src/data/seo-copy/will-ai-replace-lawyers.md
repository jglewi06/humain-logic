# Will AI replace lawyers?

## The short answer
Not any time soon. In our job tracker, lawyers are rated **"steady,"** with **low hiring pressure by 2030**. AI is very good at some legal tasks, like searching case law and drafting a first version of a document. But the heart of the job, advising clients, exercising judgment, arguing in court and carrying professional responsibility, still needs a licensed person.

## What AI is changing in legal work
Research and drafting are the parts most exposed to AI. That's why early pressure falls mostly on **junior research and document work**, not on experienced lawyers. Stanford's Digital Economy Lab reports that experienced lawyers show little job loss so far ([Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, Aug 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2026/08/Canaries_August2026.pdf)). Paralegals, whose work is more routine, sit in our "shift" group.

There's also an important caution: AI tools can invent citations that look real. Courts have sanctioned lawyers for filing briefs with fake AI-generated cases, so every AI draft has to be checked.

## Our estimate and where it comes from
Our automation timeline for lawyers is **2050–2070, with low confidence**. That is a HumAIn Logic editorial estimate, not a figure from the researchers we cite. Advocacy, judgment and licensing rules all slow full automation. Read how we build these ranges on our [methodology page](/methodology), and see [Metaculus's labor forecasting hub](https://www.metaculus.com/labor-hub/jobs/lawyers-and-law-clerks/) for another view.

## What lawyers can do now
- Learn legal AI tools carefully, and always verify citations.
- Understand your ethics duties around confidentiality, competence and supervision when using AI.
- Consider AI, privacy and data law, which are growing practice areas.
- Double-check AI output with free sources like the [Cornell Legal Information Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/).

## FAQ
**Will AI replace lawyers?** Unlikely in the foreseeable future. Our tracker rates lawyers "steady," with pressure mostly on junior research work.
**Which legal jobs are most affected?** Paralegals and entry-level document review face more pressure than experienced attorneys.
**Can I trust AI for legal research?** Use it for a first pass only. AI can make up cases, so verify every citation.
**Should law students worry?** It's worth learning AI tools and building client and courtroom skills that AI can't copy.
