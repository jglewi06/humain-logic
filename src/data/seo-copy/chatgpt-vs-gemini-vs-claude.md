# ChatGPT vs Gemini vs Claude: which AI assistant is right for you?

ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude are the three AI assistants people ask us about most. All three are genuinely useful, all three have free plans, and all three can make mistakes. The honest answer to "which one is best?" is: it depends on what you do all day and which apps you already use.

This page compares them fairly, in plain English. Prices and plan names change often, so we've dated everything.

## The quick answer

- **Pick ChatGPT** if you want one all-round helper for writing, questions, images and voice chat, with the biggest community of tips and tutorials.
- **Pick Gemini** if you live in Google's world (Gmail, Docs, Drive, Android) and want an assistant that fits into those apps.
- **Pick Claude** if you work with long documents, reports or careful writing and want a thoughtful writing partner.

You don't have to choose forever. Every one of them has a free plan, so you can try all three on the same task this afternoon.

## ChatGPT

ChatGPT, from OpenAI, is the assistant most people try first. You type or speak a request and it replies in plain language. It helps with emails, summaries, brainstorming and explanations, and it can read files you upload, create images and search the web ([our ChatGPT listing](/tools/chatgpt)).

**What it does well.** It's very versatile, so one tool covers writing, explaining, planning and summarizing. The free plan is useful for everyday chats, and it works on the web, iPhone and Android, including voice conversations. Because so many people use it, tutorials and example prompts are easy to find.

**Things to keep in mind.** It can state wrong facts with total confidence, so double-check anything important. The most capable models and higher limits require a paid plan. As with any assistant, avoid pasting private or sensitive information.

**Pricing (Oct 15, 2026):** Free; Go $8/month; Plus $20/month; Pro from $100/month ([OpenAI pricing](https://chatgpt.com/pricing)).

## Gemini

Gemini is Google's AI assistant. It answers questions and helps you write and plan, and, if you allow it, works with your Google apps such as Gmail, Drive and Docs. Paid Google AI plans add more powerful models, video generation and higher limits ([our Gemini listing](/tools/gemini)).

**What it does well.** It's free with a Google account and has a low-cost entry plan. Its biggest strength is fitting into tools you may already use: finding an old email, summarizing a Doc, or drafting in Gmail. It's available on Android, iPhone and the web.

**Things to keep in mind.** Its most helpful features depend on giving it access to your Google data, which is a personal choice worth thinking about. Google's plan names and tiers change often, which can be confusing. And like all chatbots, its answers need checking.

**Pricing (Oct 15, 2026, US):** Free; Google AI Plus $4.99/month; Google AI Pro $19.99/month; Google AI Ultra from $99.99/month ([Google pricing](https://gemini.google/subscriptions/)).

## Claude

Claude is made by Anthropic. It's especially good at reading and summarizing long documents, writing clear reports and working through files you share. You can use it on the web, desktop or phone ([our Claude listing](/tools/claude)).

**What it does well.** It handles long documents and files well, and its writing tends to sound natural and well organized. The free plan includes web search and file creation, and paid plans add connections to other apps and tools.

**Things to keep in mind.** The free plan has tighter usage limits than paid tiers, and heavy daily users may find themselves pushed toward the pricier Max plan. It can make mistakes too, so fact-check.

**Pricing (Oct 15, 2026):** Free; Pro $20/month ($17/month billed annually, $200 up front); Max from $100/month ([Anthropic pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)).

## What they all have in common

It's easy to focus on differences, but the similarities matter more for everyday use:

- **All three can be wrong.** They predict likely-sounding answers, so they sometimes invent facts, quotes or sources. Check anything that matters, especially health, money and legal questions.
- **All three have free plans** that are enough for most people to start.
- **All three improve quickly.** New model versions arrive every few weeks, so "which is smartest" rankings go stale fast.
- **Privacy is your call.** Each company publishes settings for chat history and whether your conversations are used for training. Look in settings before sharing anything personal. Our [staying safe with AI guide](/guides/staying-safe-with-ai) walks through it.

## How to pick in 10 minutes

1. Pick one real task from your week, for example "turn these meeting notes into a short email."
2. Paste the same request into ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude (free plans are fine).
3. Ask yourself: which answer needed the least fixing? Which one sounded most like me?
4. Keep that one. Only pay if you hit the free limits regularly.

If you use Gmail and Docs all day, give Gemini an extra look. If you mostly work with long PDFs or reports, give Claude an extra look. If you want one app for everything, including images and voice, ChatGPT is a safe default.

## FAQ

**Which is better, ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude?**
None is best for everyone. ChatGPT is the most all-round, Gemini fits best with Google apps, and Claude is strong with long documents and writing. Try all three free.

**Which one is cheapest?**
All three have free plans. As of October 15, 2026, Gemini's cheapest paid plan (Google AI Plus, $4.99/month) is lower than ChatGPT Go ($8/month) and Claude Pro ($20/month, or $17/month billed annually).

**Is ChatGPT Plus worth it?**
Only if you regularly hit free-plan limits or need the more capable models. Many people never need to pay.

**Which is best for writing?**
Many people like Claude's natural tone for longer pieces, but ChatGPT and Gemini are also good. Test with your own writing.

**Are they safe to use with private information?**
Be careful. Check each app's privacy settings and avoid sharing passwords, financial details or other people's private information.
