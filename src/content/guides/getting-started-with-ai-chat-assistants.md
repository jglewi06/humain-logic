AI chat assistants like ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Microsoft Copilot can feel a little intimidating at first. What do you type? Will you "break" it? Here's the reassuring truth: you can't break it, and talking to an AI assistant is a lot like giving instructions to a helpful new colleague. The clearer you are, the better the help you get.

## Step 1: Pick one assistant to start

All of the big assistants have free versions, and any of them is a fine place to begin:

- **ChatGPT** (OpenAI): a popular all-rounder
- **Claude** (Anthropic): strong with long documents and careful writing
- **Gemini** (Google): handy if you use Gmail and Google Docs
- **Microsoft Copilot**: built into Windows and Microsoft 365

Choose one, create an account, and stick with it for a couple of weeks while you learn. You can always try others later. (See our tool pages for current free and paid plans.)

## Step 2: Understand what a "prompt" is

A **prompt** is simply what you type (or say) to the AI. It can be a question, a request or a set of instructions. There's no special code or magic words. Plain, everyday language works best.

## Step 3: Use the "four-part" recipe

The companies that make these tools publish advice on writing good prompts, and it comes down to a few common ideas: be clear and specific, give context and say what you want the result to look like ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-engineering); [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview)). An easy way to remember it:

1. **Task**: What do you want? ("Write an email...")
2. **Context**: What's the background? ("...to my landlord about a leaking kitchen tap that's been broken for two weeks...")
3. **Tone or role**: How should it sound? ("...polite but firm...")
4. **Format**: What should the result look like? ("...under 150 words, with a clear subject line.")

### Before and after

**Vague prompt:**
> Write an email to my landlord.

**Clear prompt:**
> Write a polite but firm email to my landlord asking them to fix a leaking kitchen tap. It's been leaking for two weeks and I reported it once already on May 3. Keep it under 150 words and include a subject line.

The second version gives the AI everything it needs, so you'll spend less time fixing the result.

## Step 4: Have a conversation

You don't have to get it perfect the first time. The real power is in the follow-up. Try:

- "Make it shorter."
- "Make it sound friendlier."
- "Give me three other options."
- "Explain that like I'm new to the topic."
- "Put that in a table."
- "What questions should I ask a professional about this?"

Treat it like a back-and-forth with an assistant who's happy to revise as many times as you like.

## Helpful prompt starters

Here are some everyday prompts to try:

- **Explaining:** "Explain what a 401(k) is in simple terms, with an example."
- **Summarizing:** "Summarize this article in five bullet points: [paste text]."
- **Planning:** "Create a 7-day meal plan for two people on a budget, with a shopping list."
- **Writing:** "Help me write a thank-you note to my child's teacher."
- **Learning:** "Quiz me with 5 questions on basic Spanish greetings."
- **Deciding:** "Help me compare two job offers. Ask me questions first."

That last one is a great trick: **ask the AI to ask *you* questions** first. It helps it understand what you really need.

## Step 5: Give it examples

If you want something in a particular style, show it. For example: "Here's a social post I liked: [example]. Write three more like it about our weekend bakery special." Both OpenAI and Anthropic recommend examples as one of the most effective ways to guide the result ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-engineering); [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/overview)).

## Common beginner mistakes (and easy fixes)

- **Being too vague.** Add who, what, why and how long.
- **Accepting the first answer.** Ask for changes, more options or simpler wording.
- **Trusting every fact.** AI can make things up. Double-check names, numbers, dates and quotes, especially for health, money or legal topics.
- **Sharing private information.** Don't paste passwords, ID numbers or other people's personal details. Use placeholders like "[my address]".
- **Giving up too soon.** If an answer misses the mark, rephrase and try again. That's normal, not failure.

## Good to know

- **Free versions have limits.** You may hit a daily cap or get a less powerful model. That's fine for learning.
- **Some assistants can search the web**, which helps with recent news, but still check the links.
- **You can usually delete chats** or turn off chat history in the settings.
- **Voice mode** on phone apps lets you just talk, which many beginners find easier.

Try this today:

1. Ask your assistant to explain something you've always wondered about.
2. Ask a follow-up: "Can you give me a real-life example?"
3. Ask it to turn the answer into a short checklist.
4. Check one fact from the answer on a trusted website.

That's it. You've just practiced the core skills: asking clearly, refining and verifying.

## The takeaway

Good prompting isn't a technical skill. It's clear communication. Say what you want, give some background, describe the result you'd like, and keep the conversation going. With a little practice, an AI assistant can become one of the most useful tools you have.
